## What we learnt

**E9 — trail trust is a latency dial, not a robustness dial.** In plain terms: how much an ant
trusts a trail does **not** decide whether a lie works — it decides how *long* the lie takes to
work. 768 twin runs, trail trust *n* ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12} × evaporation
rate {0.003, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02} × 16 seeds.

- **There is no tipping point.** We predicted that below some level of trust, lies simply would not
  propagate — and that above it they suddenly would. The opposite happened: p_capture is *highest* at low n (69% at n=1)
  and falls to a ~47% plateau for n ≥ 2. Monotone decreasing where the pitchfork predicts
  increasing.
- **The bimodality is not caused by the non-linearity.** `read` and `captured` stay the same event
  at *every* exponent, including n = 0.5 and n = 1 where the rule is sub-linear or linear. Only
  9 of the 704 non-degenerate runs landed in the middle band. The literature critique's "canonical pitchfork under the
  Deneubourg rule" explanation **does not hold for this system**, and H7 is promoted back from
  artifact to finding. What does produce it is now open — best guess is that the colony shares one
  field, so it has one state variable, not N independent choices.
- **Trust is not a trade-off.** Going from n = 1 to n = 3 roughly quadruples food collected
  (median 120 → 448) *while* lowering capture probability (69% → 50%). Over the useful range,
  trusting trails more is free on both counts — which nobody expected.
- **What n actually buys is time:** time-to-capture rises **141 → 566 ticks** from n=1 to n=12, a
  4× delay, and it is the only measure that moves cleanly with the exponent. High n does not stop a
  false trace taking the colony, it makes the colony *slow* to be taken. That is a detection
  window, and it is the useful security reading of the parameter.
- **p_capture is flat in evaporation at every exponent** (at n=1: 11, 10, 11, 12 of 16 across the
  four rates). So `evapRate` and the exponent are **separable for capture probability** —
  H3's strong form is falsified on this axis, which per `critique-response.md` is the more useful
  result: latency-based robustness can be bought without paying in reach.

**H5 — tested, underpowered, undecided.** The (evaporation × fidelity) performance surface was run
at 4 × 11 × 16. The fidelity main effect is large and real (57 → 448 median food, saturating at
n ≈ 2.5–4). The evaporation main effect (228 → 358) and the ridge interaction both sit inside the
within-cell seed spread. Not a falsification — the instrument cannot resolve it. See the correction
below for why.

## What we learnt — the colony-size sweep and the nulls

`results/horizon-antsweep.json`, 1440 runs, every arm paired on seed so the maze and the food
position are identical across colony sizes and across stigmergy levels.

- **Colony size sets the coordination horizon.** At 60–89 cells, exploitation goes 0% (N=10) → 10%
  → 25% → 85% → 100% (N=160). The old `D_max = 1/(8·evapRate)` contains no N at all and cannot
  produce this. The single-trace model is dead.
- **The interaction prediction was wrong, and the replacement is better.** We predicted N would help
  *most* at fast decay. It doesn't. What happens instead: **evaporation binds only when traffic is
  scarce.** At N=40, exploitation falls 67% → 33% as decay speeds up. At N=160 the decay rate varies
  6.7× and the result is identical — 100% at every rate. That explains the compression that was the
  central puzzle: λ varies 6.7× but at realistic colony sizes the horizon is traffic-limited.
- **The nulls work, and the null is linear.** Median food delivered at 35–89 cells: no-trails gives
  2 / 6 / 12 / 24 / 46 as N doubles — almost exactly linear, which is what independent searchers
  must do and is a clean check that the baseline behaves as a baseline.
- **Stigmergy gives superlinear returns to scale.** Full stigmergy on the same seeds:
  5 / 12 / 44 / 326 / 499. Going from 40 to 80 ants multiplies food by **7.4×**.
  > Stigmergy converts linear returns to scale into superlinear. The coordination horizon is the
  > distance at which that conversion stops working.
- **Recruitment is the mechanism, not navigation.** The middle arm — home trail only, so ants can
  navigate home but nobody advertises a find — stays roughly linear (5 / 9 / 22 / 67 / 151). The
  superlinearity appears only when the food trail is switched on.

## Corrections made along the way — do not re-make these

- **The adversarial experiments were measuring the wrong thing, and the user caught it.** Every
  impulse run injects with `field.add()`, which bypasses the ant's deposit tank: ~50 deposits'
  worth of influence (`DEPOSIT_RATE = 20`) delivered instantly by an attacker that never walked
  there and paid nothing. The thesis says influence is bounded *because* an attacker must travel
  and spend. So the instrument contradicted the claim by construction. Those results are a
  **free-attacker control**, not evidence about bounded influence.
- **Following the log instead of the thesis.** Day 1's "start here" named E9 on the impulse tool,
  so E9 got run — even though the centre of the programme had already moved to the horizon and the
  instrument no longer matched the question. Check the next action against the model, not just
  against the last log.
- **Documents were being produced faster than anyone could read them.** Nine research docs for two
  days of work is how you lose track of your own project. `research/` is now four files; the rest
  is in `research/archive/` with a README. Findings go in chat; a doc gets written when it is asked
  for.
- **We had four names for one number** ("trail fidelity", "choice exponent", "follow weight",
  "follow exponent"). Canonical name is now **trail trust**. One name per concept, in
  `research/glossary.md`.
- **`odor()` fires only on the exact food cell** — there is no gradient at range. So "discovery"
  in stigsim *is* random search, which is why it was 100% at every distance: 40 ants over 6000
  ticks cover a 31×31 maze regardless of how far the food is. That is a coverage fact, not a
  finding about stigmergy, and it is what makes a no-trail colony the honest baseline.
- **Ratios of tiny numbers lie.** At N=10–20 in the far field the arms deliver 2 vs 1 food; the
  ratio reads "2.5×" and means nothing. Quote the absolute numbers at small N.
- **N=160 is at the ceiling** (499 of 500 delivered), so the far end of the N curve is a lower
  bound, not a measurement. Fix with longer routes or a shorter tick budget before quoting it.
- **Distance bins are not matched on maze structure.** Distance is measured from whatever layout
  each seed produced, so reading *across* a distance row mixes in other differences. Reading *down*
  a column — across N, across stigmergy level — is paired on seed and clean.
- **`D_max = 1/(8·evapRate)` answers a different question than λ = 1/(4·evapRate).** The first
  requires a deposit to survive a *round trip*, the second a one-way journey. Same shape, factor of
  two apart. Mixing them up was part of the confusion.

- **`layout: "random"` makes the seed move the food, so absolute-performance sweeps are swamped.**
  At n=4 the 16 seeds span IQR 40–560 food; every cell-to-cell difference on the surface is smaller
  than that. Seed 3 collects ~1 food at *every* setting, seed 6 collects ~600. Fix the food layout
  across seeds, or use several hundred seeds, before reading any absolute-yield surface. The twin-
  run impulse design does not have this problem because both twins share a layout — **measure
  differences, not absolutes.**
- **8 seeds is still too few; use 16 minimum.** The same n=5 configuration read 25% capture on
  seeds 1–8 and 47% on seeds 1–16. Day 1's "do not report below ~8 seeds" was not conservative
  enough for a proportion near 0.5 (binomial SE at p=0.5, n=16 is still ±12.5 points).
- **n = 0 is a degenerate control, not a data point.** With both follow weights zero, `scoreCell`
  skips every channel and returns 1 uniformly — and because odor is added *inside* the power, the
  ant cannot smell food either. It is a blind random walk (still 79 food by t=5500). Its
  p_capture = 0 is trivially true and says nothing about linear recruitment.
- **High p_capture at low n is not a hardened colony.** It is a colony with no trail structure to
  capture: a 1000-unit blob is the only feature in an otherwise flat field, so everyone goes to it.
  Read p_capture against yield, never alone.
- Follow weights are **half-steps only** (`isHalfStep`, `deterministicPow` throws otherwise),
  bounded by `MAX_TRAIL_POWER = 32`, with the row's Σ|w| also bounded by 32. So n = 0.5 is the
  smallest non-degenerate exponent — there is nothing to sample between 0 and 0.5.
- The `--follow` patch was verified by **exact seed-for-seed reproduction** of
  `results/impulse-empty-8seeds.json` as the n=5, seeds-1–8 sub-row of the new plane. Do this on
  every harness change; it is cheap and it is what makes the old file still citable.

### What we learnt

**Experiment 2 — pheromone mass is not the channel.** Written up as §4b of `model.md` and rung 8 of
the artifact. Tripling every ant's deposition is a perfect null (median difference 0 food, 35 seeds
better / 35 worse, n=96); at matched total deposition the bigger colony wins 57/19 and 64/11. At
60–74 cells, 3× louder takes 32 food to 48; 3× more ants takes 32 to 498.

**Experiment 1 — there is no single D\*; the bonus is a ridge.** §4d of `model.md`. The distance at
which stigmergy pays most moves outward with colony size: 35 cells at 96 ants, 45 at 192, 55 at 384.

**Feinerman & Korman, *The ANTS problem* (arXiv:1701.02555)** — reviewed. It is the formal theory of
our *null arm*: k agents, central nest, **no communication after the start**, expected time
Θ(D + D²/k), and log log k bits of prior knowledge about colony size needed for optimal speed-up.
Useful framings it gives us, and one trap:

- Their theorem is a ceiling on the no-trail arm. Our superlinearity is not in tension with it — it
  measures throughput with a channel their model forbids. Have that answer ready; someone will ask.
- Their "advice assigned at the nest before the run" is *exactly* `mode.config.doctrines[i]`. Which
  gives a principled reason for the norm against live doctrine edits: a mid-run `set-doctrine` is
  out-of-band communication.
- **Not a security source.** Their adversary places the treasure; it does not lie. Zhong & Evans and
  Aswale et al. remain the right citations for that.
- Bampas et al. 2023, *Treasure hunt with volatile pheromones*, is now the top of the reading list:
  ANTS-style formalism *with* evaporating traces, i.e. the formal object sitting exactly between the
  two arms we measure.

### Corrections made along the way — do not re-make these

- **The deposit tank is a budget, not a rate, and it silently breaks deposition experiments.**
  `DEPOSITS_PER_CELL = 3` × `DEPOSIT_RATE 20` × gain, against `tankMax` 6400, is **106 cells of
  trail per leg**. Turning the lay gain up therefore does *not* make an ant louder — it makes it
  louder over `106/gain` cells and silent after, so the trail stops short of the nest. Any loudness
  experiment must scale `tankMax` by the same factor. (`MAX_LAY_GAIN = 3`, integers only, so 3× is
  the widest ratio available.)
- **Standing field mass must be sampled before the source is depleted.** Once it runs out no ant can
  refill at food, a searching ant never re-enters the returning state, it goes dry at 106 cells and
  stops laying — so a colony that *wins* reads as low pheromone mass. The first version of
  `traffic.ts` sampled at 0.75 × ticks and read a winning colony as unmatched.
- **A set distance is only set if the maze is that big.** `distance.ts` silently places food at the
  furthest reachable cell when the request exceeds the maze's reach. A branchy (`loopRate` 0.12)
  31×31 maze tops out near 70 cells, so a sweep asking for 90/110/140 returned **three identical
  rows**. Cost one full sweep. The tool now flags it; `scaling.py` drops affected distances.
- **A power law was the wrong model, and fitting one anyway produced a plausible wrong number.** The
  full arm's break-even exponent moved 1.48 → 1.83 with the delivery target. Unstable exponent =
  wrong functional form, not a noisy estimate.
- **Pooling across colony size averaged a ridge into a line.** One bonus-vs-distance curve fits at
  R² = 0.19 and extrapolates D\* = 346 cells, 7× beyond the measured range. The per-N structure was
  the finding; the pooled fit destroyed it. `scaling.py` now refuses the pooled fit.
- **`ls`, `find` and `grep` go through the rtk proxy and can be actively misleading.** A 0-byte file
  was missing from `ls` output while `lsof` showed it open; `grep -n` reported "0 matches in 0
  files" for strings that were present. **Verify anything load-bearing with `python3 -c`**, not with
  the shell text tools.
- **Another session was editing the same files concurrently.** `model.md` was rewritten and the
  artifact was republished mid-task; the first artifact publish was refused as out-of-date, and an
  earlier `model.md` edit of ours was overwritten. Both recovered by re-reading and merging. **Check
  the current state of a shared file immediately before editing it**, not at the start of the task.

### What we learnt

- **The ridge was the food cap.** In `breakeven-b2` the full arm sat at the 2,500 cap in 59–100% of

- **The far side of the horizon is a time budget.** At 55 cells the bonus for N=16 reads 2.5× at

- **Evaporation is not the lever.** 4× slower decay: paired wins negative in 13/15 far cells and on

- **What N buys is time to establish, plus a floor for holding the trail at all.** Small colonies
  failing at 6–12 junctions in 6000 ticks reach ~100% of ceiling in the final third of 18000, with
  the same late bonus as big colonies — except **12 junctions at 10 ants**, which stays at 9% of
  ceiling with trails forming and collapsing (18/24 seeds show a transient take-off).
